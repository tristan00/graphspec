A module imports only its own group, `infra/` and the groups below it; `tests/test_standing.py` holds the order. `infra/` imports nothing else of `graphspec` and nothing of `tuning`.

Domain-free machinery that reads a typed temporal truth graph from staged Parquet or an optional LadybugDB database and derives the graph variants a model search explores. Structure comes from the schema a task file states, documented in [the contract](../docs/contract.md). Mechanism lives in the `graphspec.infra` package: files and arrays, database access and hashing.

Set an explicit absolute `GRAPHSPEC_HOME` before importing the package or launching Python. There is no default home or profile configuration. On Windows, output must resolve to that runtime volume; managed caches and temporary files stay beneath the home. The checkout launcher accepts mandatory `-Home`; see the [storage and loader guide](../docs/loader.md).

**Truth graph and views.** A bank of version 18 stores one graph as `stored.Facts`, facts with the interval in which each holds; [the contract](../docs/contract.md) lists its arrays. Steps are a view the pipeline chooses by `time/steps` and `time/several`. A view is first shown as stated, a `graph.Shown`, and then encoded, with the encoders fitted for its statement, into a `graph.Graph`, spans over the steps of the view, so an unchanged observation or fact costs one row however long it is shown:

- `kinds` and `entity_token` (the 64-bit hash of `type:key`) per entity, and per step `period`, `step_time`, the time the step shows, and `step_token`, the 64-bit hash of the name of the graph
- observation spans `obs_entity`, `obs_start`, `obs_stop` (exclusive), `obs_values` and `obs_x`: an entity's maximal run of consecutive steps with identical stated values, the values as stated and as encoded; within a step observations are ordered by entity
- fact spans `fact_source`, `fact_target`, `fact_code`, `fact_start`, `fact_stop`, `fact_values`: a maximal run of consecutive steps at which the same fact with the same attributes is present, and `fact_rank`, which with the patches `order_step`, `order_position`, `order_span` reproduces the `seq` order of facts within every step exactly
- removals `removal_source`, `removal_target`, `removal_code`, `removal_step`, `removal_values`: the absent facts, in their `seq` order
- tasks `task_step`, `task_key`, `task_kind`, `task_values`, `task_x`, `task_signals`, the members of their references `member_task`, `member_name`, `member_place`, `member_source`, `member_target`, `member_code`, targets `label_task`, `label_row`, and `graph_values`, the attributes of the graph

`verify` checks a view, in time linear in the spans: steps in range, a period for every step, canonical relation codes, tasks in the order of their steps, observation spans of one entity that neither overlap nor could merge, present facts observed at every step of their span, the nodes that the references of a task name observed at its step where its kind states that an absent node stops the work, absent facts observed at or before theirs, and tokens that match the keys. The encoded values are checked where they are made, against the limit of 16 bits. A bank build refuses two keys of one type that share a token.

The stages work on `graph.Dense`, the per-step expansion: rows reference their features and attributes (`obs_ref`, `fact_ref`) in `x_table` and `value_table` instead of copying them, so a stage that copies or filters rows moves integers. `step_graph` names the graph of the group every step came from, `step_time` and `step_token` travel with the steps through joins, cuts and stitching, and `entity_connected` whether an entity may appear in a later segment.

**Draws.** Every random choice of an operation or a representation is a number drawn by `infra.hashing.unit(seed, salt, *keys)`: the seed the derivation is given, a salt naming the draw, and keys of identity only: the entity's token (`Segment.tokens`), for an edge the tokens of its two ends, ordered where the edge is read symmetrically, and the hash of its relation's name, the graph's name from `step_token`, and for a draw made per step the time from `step_time`. No draw reads a position in an array, an entity number, a step number, a worker, a device or a random generator. A draw that picks one of several candidates takes the candidate with the largest weight plus a Gumbel number drawn from the candidate's own keys, and candidates are ordered by their keys wherever a tie or a cut depends on their order. `infra.hashing.hashed(*keys)` gives the 64-bit identity of a row of keys, as for the events of the walks. A generator of options draws at the build in the same way, by `Seen.unit`: the seed of its statement, its name as the salt, the name of the graph, the key of the task of its decision, and keys of what it draws, the content of an option or the key of the option it changes with the copy, the place of the action and the column.

**One engine.** Every variant is derived in segments, and a whole graph is one segment. `segments.opened` prepares a segment: the steps of one graph from a step to another (`edits.cut`, which keeps the rows of the tables they read), an edit applied to them, its entities numbered by the rank of their number in the derivation, so every sort and tie-break matches the whole, while draws receive the keys of entities and the time and graph of steps. An entity may appear in a later segment when it is connected across the graphs of a group or its graph continues after the segment; only those are carried. `transform.derive` runs the steps on the segment, each with the state its operation carried from the segment before: window copies still open, running degree and activity counts, fact runs, orphan states, derived facts live at the boundary, typed relations still live, the group of the last period; a phase that brings carried entities into a segment widens it (`Segment.widened`). `representations.base.represented` then gives the segment's arrays, with the state of the base (last observations, replay states, counts, the groups of periods and a halo of recent rows for the lookbacks bounded in time: event DAG, boundaries, walks) and of each representation (coupling tails, mixed chains). Every segment's rows are written with the numbers of the derivation and offsets, then stitched into the variant, which is bitwise equal to deriving and representing the whole graph, or the physical join of a group, in one segment.

`segments.variant` runs the segments of a variant in order in the calling process; pieces stay in memory up to 64 MiB and are then written to the variant's files as they come (`stitch.Appender`), so a group's size is bounded by disk, not by memory. A variant held in memory is written as one file, `segments.pack`, and the arrays computed over every segment as another, `stitched.pack`; one written as it comes holds one `.npy` file per array; `graphspec.infra.files.Arrays` reads both. When a split has few groups, `schedule.variants` spreads the same work over worker processes: every phase of every step is a stage, run in order on the segments of a group where it carries state, on any segment whose input is ready where it carries none, and part by part where it runs in parts (the filters of `graphspec.edges`, one part per relation), so that `graphspec.derive` computes its steps in parallel across segments. A stage's carried state is small, so it is written beside the spill files after every segment and any idle worker runs the next segment of any stage; only the representations stay on one worker, which appends every segment's arrays to the group's variant. The arrays are equal for any number of workers.

An operation may add kinds of node, relations and columns (see the contract, What an operation adds); the derivation is then given the registry of its pipeline, `pipeline.grown(registry, steps, states)`. `segments.Plan` numbers the nodes of every added kind before the entities of its graphs, one per kind and token across the graphs of a joined group, connected where the derivation holds more than one graph, and `segments.opened` widens the tables of values of each segment to the registry. Rows of values an operation or an edit adds are marked with no place in the derivation (`carry.x_global`, `carry.value_global` below zero) until `segments.placed` gives them places after every row of the graphs and of the segments before, once the steps of the segment ran and before its representations, in the calling process and on the worker of the representations alike; where a row sits depends on the cuts of segments, what every observation and fact reads does not.

`segments.Derivation` holds a derivation as a value: `begun` states the initial state of every step and representation, `continued` derives from its step to a later one and returns a new derivation that holds it as its parent, and `forked` continues it once per edit. A fork shares the segments and the state of its history and holds only what it derived and what its edits added, so the history of every fork at one step is derived once; `arrays` stitches the variant of a chain of derivations and `derived` its derived graph.

**Relation vocabulary.** `declaration.registry` numbers node types and base relations in the order the schema states them, the relations an edge table takes from a column in sorted order at the place of their table, then declares every relation a spec can derive. A relation stated without direction is symmetric. A directed relation has a canonical and a reverse code; a symmetric relation has one.

A table whose relations are taken from a column is one table with a value. Each value keeps its own code, which facts, references and events carry and which models read through a learned embedding; everything else is the table's: its parameters under `relations/<table>/` and `edges/<table>/`, its projections, its array `attributes_<table>`, and the weights models hold per relation. `Registry.groups` names, for every relation stated, every table of relations taken from a column and every relation of a set edge, the relations it holds; `Registry.coded` their codes, with every derived relation alone; `Registry.grouped` the index of the weights of every code, one per table and direction; `Registry.valued` the tables whose relations carry values, then the relations an operation added with values, `Registry.value_group` the place of each code among them, and `Registry.attributed` the most values a relation holds. `Registry.declared` names the kinds of node and of set edge the bank declares, which per-kind parameters read; `Registry.nodes` adds the kinds a pipeline added. What grows with the number of values of a column is the declaration of the registry, its tables indexed by code, and the embedding tables of relations in models.

| `<relation>` | as stated | A base relation, from an edge table. |
| `project:<table>:<side>:<A>:<B>` | yes | Entities of types A and B on one side of a relation that share a counterpart at a step. One per relation stated, table of relations taken from a column or relation of a set edge, side and pair of types whose FROM/TO pairs share a counterpart type. |
| `complete:<A>:<B>` | yes | Pairs of completed entities of types A and B at a step. |

| `diffusion` | no | Top-k graph-diffusion neighbours. |
| `rewire` | yes | Edges added by curvature or spectral rewiring. |
| `untyped` | no | Every base relation collapsed into one. |
| `temporal` | yes | An entity's link to its own earlier observation. |
| `boundary:first`, `boundary:last`, `boundary:both` | yes | Links from the first, last or only observation or event of an earlier period. |

The markers `non_relation` and `period_context` take codes above every relation. The registry extras record:

- whether a kind of task is seen as an event
- the signal width
- the base relations
- whether a step can show several facts about one entity, and the graphs that hold no task

`declaration()` serialises everything and `fingerprint()` hashes it. The bank manifest, the run plan and every trial's `selection.json` pin that hash.

**Pipeline and settings.** A configuration states its pipeline, the operations in order with their parameters, and its settings, a flat dictionary of the rest: forms of storage, steps, joining, how models read kinds and directed relations, the parameters of the representation groups a model reads, and those read when packing; [the contract](../docs/contract.md) states both. `parameters.settings(registry, groups)` generates the settings for one registry and the representation groups a model reads; an operation generates its own parameters. Each parameter is active only under its condition; `nodes/<type>/min_degree`, for example, needs `nodes/<type>/filter` set to degree or total_degree.

- `draw` walks the parameters in order and asks for a value for each active one.
- `canonical` refuses a missing active parameter, an inactive or unknown one, and an out-of-range value.
- `digest` is the SHA-256 of the sorted JSON.
- Every draw of the operations and representations is keyed by the seed the derivation is given, for the variants of a bank the `derivation_seed` the statement of the fit or search states, which the key of every variant holds, with a salt per draw, so changing one parameter never reshuffles the random choices of another.
- `materialized` drops the settings read only when packing: `time/history`, `time/context`, `neighbors/order` and `neighbors/decay`. A cached variant is named by the key the contract states: the schema, the source, the pipeline with the version of every operation, the settings, the groups, what the splits may see, the device, the seed, the versions of the code of views, derivation and representations, and the name and version of every registered entry the variant reads; no file is hashed.

**Operations.** `transform.derive` applies the steps of a pipeline to a segment in the order it states; each operation reads its parameters through the settings of [the contract](../docs/contract.md), the graph the step before it produced and, where it takes two graphs, the rows of its second graph at the steps of the segment (`state.Second`, which `segments.opened` cuts with the segment). A step that changes nothing returns its input, whose schema is not checked again, and every array a step leaves unchanged is shared with its input; in the calling process the segment is verified once, after the last step, where a step changed it or an edit was applied, a segment left as its verified view gave it not being verified again; on workers it is verified after the last step whatever the steps did, and before its representations where the pipeline holds no step; a relation whose edge filters sit at their identity values is left untouched. The seven shipped operations follow, numbered in the order the fixture of 1.2 applied them; a pipeline may state any of them, in any order, and one more than once. Rows marked as settings belong to the settings, not to an operation.

3. Nodes

Every unprotected type has its own switch and filter.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `graphs/join` | separate, single | A setting, as are the three below. separate transforms every truth graph on its own. single joins truth graphs in groups of `graphs/join_size` onto one timeline: graphs follow each other in time, periods keep increasing across them, labels and splits keep their rows, and entities of connected types with the same key become one entity so graphs meet through the entities they share. A connected entity, and a fact between two connected entities, carries across the boundaries between graphs under every stage's rules; every other entity and fact ends with its own graph. `every` periods restart at each graph. |
| `graphs/join_size` | 2–4096 | How many truth graphs one joined graph holds. Graphs whose rows fall in the same splits are ordered by name, then observer and split, and cut into groups in that order, never by their place in the bank, so a graph-level split never mixes training and validation graphs in one joined graph and reordering the graphs of a bank leaves every group and its order equal. Offered for single. |
| `graphs/connect/<type>` | on, off | Whether entities of the type are unified by key when joining. Offered for single. |
| `graphs/connect_fraction` | 0.01–1, logarithmic | The share of keys that are unified, chosen by a draw keyed by the token of the key alone, so that every graph of the group chooses alike; the rest stay per graph. Offered for single. |
| `nodes/<type>/keep` | on, off | Removes the entities of the type and every fact that touches them; off states that the entry removes the type, so a pipeline in which a later entry names it, with no merge between them, is refused when it is stated. |
| `nodes/referenced` | keep, and remove where every kind of task keeps an absent node | What becomes of a node that a reference names and that the stage would remove at the step of the task: kept as an anchor, or removed. |
| `nodes/untyped` | on, off | A setting: gives every entity one kind in the model inputs. The operations still read types, and features are untouched. Offered with more than one node type. |
| `nodes/<type>/filter` | none, degree, total_degree, core, activity, sample | Removes observations of the type's entities that fail the filter, and the facts that lose an endpoint. Offered while the type is kept. |
| `nodes/<type>/min_degree` | 1–16 | degree: an observation stays if its entity has at least that many stated incident facts among kept types at that step, a self-loop counting once. total_degree: counted over every step so far. |
| `nodes/<type>/core` | 2–16 | core: observations of every core-filtered type are removed together, repeatedly, while their incident facts to still-kept observations at that step, a self-loop counting once, number fewer than their type's threshold. Observations that failed another filter do not count. |
| `nodes/<type>/min_activity` | 2–16 | activity: an entity stays from its n-th observation, counting the copies an earlier window carried forward. |
| `nodes/<type>/sample` | 0.1–0.9 | sample: a draw keyed by the entity's key and the name of its graph keeps that fraction of the type's entities for the whole graph; in a joined group each graph draws for itself. |

With `nodes/referenced` set to keep, an observation that a reference of a task names at the step of the task is never removed; when its type is removed or it fails its type's filter, it stays as an anchor, marked in `obs_anchor`: no present fact touches it and no derivation links it, while removals of relations its entity held earlier still end at its step. Representations built on observations (coupling, events, mixed) include anchors like any other observation. The node a `link` names is not a reference: it is kept or removed as any node of its type, and a task whose link starts from or names a node the pipeline did not keep at its step, other than as an anchor, is left out (`row_left`).

4. Derived relations

Computed at each step from the present facts of the graph it is given. Projections, completion anchors, hops, diffusion and rewiring read the base relations that graph holds, as undirected pairs: a relation an earlier step removed is not read, and one a later step removes is. Projection, hops and diffusion are uses of the product of relations of `operations/paths.py`, which `graphspec.product` (15) offers as an operation: a projection is the product of a relation with its reverse through the counterparts, capped at each counterpart and kept between its two types; hops are the closure of the relations read without direction, each pair labelled by the shortest length that reaches it; diffusion is the closure of every length of the same relation, symmetric-normalised, each length weighted by the series of its kind (geometric for ppr, Poisson for heat) and computed in closed form per component, of which each entity keeps its strongest non-adjacent pairs. Completion and rewiring are not products. The facts it derives carry its state across segments as every derived relation of `paths.py` does: the runs of the relations it reads, for the cap, and the pairs live at the end of a segment, for their absent rows.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `derive/project:<table>:<side>:<A>:<B>` | on, off | Adds that projection. Offered for every projection the bank declares. |
| `derive/project_cap` | 0–64 | Only each counterpart's `cap` most recently started relations of the step take part, counting parallel facts once, relations started at one step ranked by the token of the member; 0 takes all. Offered when a projection is on. |
| `derive/complete/<type>` | on, off | Completes that type: every pair of observed entities of the completed types at a step is linked. Offered for every type. |
| `derive/complete_within/<type>` | on, off | Links completed entities only when they share a neighbour of that type; with several types on, a shared neighbour of any of them is enough. Offered while a relation of the bank joins this type to a completed type. |
| `derive/hops` | 1–3 | Adds `hop:2` up to `hop:k`. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `derive/diffusion` | none, ppr, heat | Scores pairs by personalised PageRank α(I−(1−α)T)⁻¹ or the heat kernel exp(−t(I−T)), with T the symmetric-normalised adjacency of the step's observed entities. Each entity links to its highest-scoring non-adjacent entities in its own connected component. Computed per component in float64; the infra layer chooses the device by component size. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `derive/diffusion_top_k` | 1–16 | Neighbours per entity. The scores of a component are computed with its entities in the order of their tokens, and equal scores are cut in that order. Offered with diffusion. |
| `derive/ppr_alpha` | 0.05–0.5 | α. Offered with ppr. |
| `derive/heat_time` | 0.5–5 | t. Offered with heat. |
| `derive/rewiring` | none, sdrf, fosr | fosr works inside connected components. Each addition goes to the component with the smallest spectral gap, at the non-edge that minimises the product of degree-scaled second-eigenvector entries of the component's self-looped normalised adjacency. sdrf takes the edge of lowest balanced Forman curvature. It adds an edge between the two neighbourhoods, sampled by softmax of temperature × curvature gain with Gumbel numbers drawn from the keys of the pair's ends, the time and graph of the step and the iteration, and removes the most curved edge above the threshold. Both order the entities of a step by their keys, so ties go to the first by key. Curvature runs in a numba kernel, and fosr reuses a component's decomposition until an edge changes it. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `derive/rewiring_budget` | 1–64 | Iterations per step. Offered with rewiring. |
| `derive/sdrf_temperature` | 1–500, log | Sampling temperature. Offered with sdrf. |
| `derive/sdrf_threshold` | 0.1–20, log | Curvature above which an edge is removed. Offered with sdrf. |

A derived pair is stated at every step it holds and gets an absent row at the step after each run ends. Base facts that rewiring removes are deleted at that step.

Derivation stops with an error once a variant's derived facts exceed the budget the infra layer sets from the machine's memory, and the time window stops the same way when the observation or fact copies it would carry exceed that budget, so a spec that completes a large type into a clique, or carries state across a long joined graph, fails its trial instead of exhausting the machine.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `relations/<table>/keep` | on, off | Removes a relation stated, every relation of a table taken from a column, or a relation of a set edge. Offered for every relation of the bank. |
| `relations/direction` | inverse, symmetric, directed | A setting: how models read directed relations: both directions with the reverse code, both directions with one code, or the stated direction only. Symmetric relations are always read both ways. Offered where the bank states a relation. |

6. Edge filters

Every relation stated, table of relations taken from a column, relation of a set edge and derived relation has its own filter under `edges/<table>/`. A base filter is offered for every relation of the bank; a derived relation's while the entry of `graphspec.derive` without a label can produce it: its projection; both of its completed types, with no anchor type on or an anchor type that relations of the bank join to both; hops of at least its distance; diffusion; or rewiring. A pipeline without that entry has no filter of a derived relation. Drop and persistence treat an edge of each value as an edge of its own; a cap counts the edges of every value of a table together.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `edges/direction` | inverse, symmetric, directed | How the filters read directed relations, as `relations/direction` says models do. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `edges/<table>/drop` | 0–0.9 | A draw keyed by the keys of a pair's ends, its relation's name and its graph's name removes that fraction of entity pairs, with all their rows. |
| `edges/<table>/persistence` | 1–8 | A present fact is kept from the p-th consecutive step it is stated; facts stated twice at one step share a position. |
| `edges/<table>/cap` | 0–64 | At most c neighbours per entity and step, with parallel facts counted once; 0 keeps all. A directed reading caps destinations, inverse caps destinations and sources separately, and symmetric caps each entity with a pair counted once. A pair stays only if it ranks within c at every capped end. |
| `edges/<table>/cap_order` | recent, random, largest:&lt;attribute&gt; | Which facts a cap keeps: latest run start, a draw keyed as the drop's, or the largest value of one of the table's attributes, then the latest run start. Pairs still equal are ranked by the tokens of their ends, ordered where the pair is read symmetrically, then by the hash of their relation's name, never by row or entity number. Offered when the cap is on. |

Relations read symmetrically count their two directions as one pair in every edge filter.

Whenever a stage removes present facts, it also removes absent rows left without any earlier kept present row. The event history therefore never removes a relation the variant never stated.

7. Forms of one relation, `graphspec.relation_forms`

Formerly `graphspec.merging`, renamed so that it is not mistaken for the merge of two graphs. In order: it collapses the types of relations, reduces parallel edges, adds reverse edges and adds self loops. Parallel edges are the facts of one relation from one node to another stated at one step, as stated, present or absent. It carries the relations made untyped that are still live, for their absent rows, and nothing else; it draws nothing; it counts nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `relation_forms/types` | typed, untyped | untyped replaces every base relation with `untyped` and drops attributes. Its absent row stays only when no underlying relation between the pair is still present. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `relation_forms/parallel` | keep, first, last, sum, mean, min, max | keep keeps every parallel edge; first and last keep the first or the last of them in the order the rule for ties, then the time they hold from, give facts about one edge; sum, mean, min and max keep the last, holding in each column the sum, mean, least or greatest of the values the present ones hold, missing values left out and missing where none holds one, in a row of values the operation adds. Facts of relations made untyped carry no values, so which of them is kept changes nothing. Offered where the bank states a relation. |
| `relation_forms/reverse` | off, on | Adds for every directed relation the bank states alone in its table a relation `<entry>.<relation>` joining its kinds the other way, holding every fact of it reversed with its values. A table of relations taken from a column is not reversed. Offered where such a relation exists and types are kept. |
| `relation_forms/loops` | off, on | Adds a relation without direction `<entry>.loops` joining every kind of node to itself, holding an edge from every node observed at a step, other than as an anchor, to itself at that step. |

The operations added after the seven follow. Each states what it requires of the schema and what it needs of a bank (`Need`, see the contract, Operations): a search holds an entry absent where the bank cannot serve it, and a pipeline holding one is refused when stated, naming what the bank lacks.

8. Merge of graphs on keys, `graphspec.merge`, which takes two graphs

The entry names its second graph, a graph of the bank shown at the steps of the graph it changes (see the contract, Second graphs). At every step the merge holds the observations and the facts of both graphs, their entities met by kind and key. Where both hold an observation of one entity, or rows about one edge (the same relation between the same two entities, in either order for a relation without direction), the step keeps those of one graph. Facts whose ends are not both observed, other than as anchors, are dropped, and absent rows left without an earlier present row are removed, as every stage that removes facts does. It carries the state of that removal and nothing else. It draws nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `merge/prefer` | first, second | Which graph keeps its rows where both hold an observation of an entity or rows about an edge at one step: the graph changed, or the second. |

9. Difference of two graphs, `graphspec.difference`, which takes two graphs

The entry names its second graph as a merge does. At every step the difference removes the present facts of the graph that the second graph also holds present at that step: the same relation between the same two entities, met by kind and key, in either order for a relation without direction. Observations are kept, and absent rows left without an earlier present row are removed. It carries the state of that removal and nothing else. It draws nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `difference/match` | keys, values | keys: an edge the second graph holds at the step is removed whatever its values; values: only where the second graph's edge carries the same values, compared as stated, bit for bit, an edge without values matching one without values. |

10. Edges computed from the columns of two nodes, `graphspec.nearby`

Adds a relation named after its entry, from nodes of a first kind to nodes of a second, whose edges hold their distance in a column it adds, `<entry>.distance`, a number encoded by the encoder the entry states and fitted as every added column is. It needs two kinds of node or of set edge that state a column of numbers both state, or one kind that states one; the distance between two nodes is the Euclidean distance between the encoded values of the columns of numbers their kinds state alike, never of columns an operation added, and stops naming the kinds where the encoders fitted for the statement give none of those columns a width. At every step it reads the nodes of the two kinds observed at the step, other than as anchors, and joins no node missing a value in one of those columns. An edge is stated at every step it is found, as stated edges are, with no absent row when it is no longer found. Under a threshold the relation has no direction and holds each pair once, the lower entity first where both ends are of one kind; under nearest it is directed, from each node to its own nearest. It carries no state from segment to segment and draws nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `nearby/kinds` | `<first>:<second>`, every two kinds that qualify | The kinds it joins, from the first to the second. |
| `nearby/rule` | threshold, nearest | threshold: every two nodes within the distance; nearest: every node of the first kind to its nearest of the second. |
| `nearby/threshold` | 0.01–100, logarithmic | The distance. Offered for threshold. |
| `nearby/k` | 1–16 | How many nearest each node joins, fewer where fewer are observed; candidates at equal distances are taken in the order of their tokens. Offered for nearest. |
| `nearby/encoder` | the encoders of numbers, no default | The encoder of the distance. |

11. Edges between consecutive rows of a group, `graphspec.consecutive`

Fills a relation the schema states without columns from a kind of node to itself, which it needs of the bank. The rows are the nodes of that kind. A row joins a group at the first step it is seen in it: with the whole graph as one group, at its first observation other than as an anchor; with a group per counterpart, at the first step at which a present edge of the grouping relation links it, on the side stated, to that counterpart, so that a row with several counterparts is in several groups. In a group, rows follow one another in the order of the time they joined, then of their tokens; each row is joined to the row before it, from the earlier to the later, at the step the later joined, where the earlier is observed at that step other than as an anchor and joined at most the gap before; a longer gap, or another graph of a joined group, starts the group again. Edges are stated at that step only, without values; a pipeline that states `graphspec.window` after it carries them on. It carries the rows that joined each group and the last row of each group from segment to segment. It draws nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `consecutive/pairing` | the relations that qualify, alone or with a grouping | `<relation>`: the rows of the whole graph form one group; `<relation>:<grouping>:<side>`: the rows are grouped by their counterparts over the relation `<grouping>`, on which they are the sources or the targets. |
| `consecutive/gap` | 1–10⁹, logarithmic, in the unit of time of the graph | The longest time between two consecutive rows that are joined. |

12. Neighbourhood of nodes to a number of hops, at a time, `graphspec.neighbourhood`

At every step it keeps the part of the graph within a number of hops of its nodes at that step, and removes the rest: the observations of the nodes reached, the present facts between two nodes reached, and the absent facts whose ends were kept at or before their step. Its nodes at a step are those the tasks of the step reference, observed at it, and, where a kind is stated, every node of the kind observed at it; a node a task references is therefore never removed at the step of the task, and a step without such nodes keeps nothing. Hops follow the present facts of the step in either direction, whatever their relation; it counts paths, and under the forms of storage of set edges counts them as stored, as `graphspec.derive` does. It carries the nodes it kept, for the absent facts of later segments, and the state of removing absent rows left without an earlier present row. It draws nothing. `neighbourhood.within(seeds, source, target, hops)` gives the keys reached from seeds over pairs of keys.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `neighbourhood/hops` | 0–3 | How many hops from its nodes; 0 keeps its nodes alone. |
| `neighbourhood/around` | references, or a kind of node | Its nodes: those the tasks of the step reference, and every node of the kind where one is stated. |

13. Measures of structure as columns, `graphspec.measures`

Adds a column per measure stated on, `<entry>.degree`, `<entry>.components` and `<entry>.cores`, numbers encoded by the encoder the entry states and fitted as every added column is, and places them on every kind of node the registry holds when it runs. At every step it measures the graph it is given: the nodes observed at the step, anchors among them, and the present facts of every relation the graph then holds, derived and added ones included, as undirected edges. The degree of a node counts the facts it is an end of, parallel facts each and a self-loop once; its component is the number of nodes its connected component holds, itself included; its core number is the largest k such that it belongs to the k-core, peeled by the same degree. Edges joining sets and edges about edges are counted as their forms of storage store them, as its counts state. Every observation receives a row of values of its own, its stated values unchanged and the measures written into the columns added. It carries no state from segment to segment and draws nothing.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `measures/degree`, `measures/components`, `measures/cores` | off, on | Which measures it adds. With none on it changes nothing. |
| `measures/encoder` | the encoders of numbers, no default | The encoder of every column it adds. Offered where a measure is on. |

14. Contraction of groups of nodes, `graphspec.contract`

Contracts the nodes of a kind into one node per group of the values one of its stated columns holds, of a kind the operation adds and names after its entry, which holds the columns of the contracted kind, those an earlier operation placed on it included. Two nodes are in one group at a step where that column holds the same encoded values for both; the nodes of the added kind are numbered before the derivation begins, from the groups the graphs given to it hold, one node per group in a joined group as in the physical join of its graphs, so a group that an edit or an earlier operation brings stops naming the node and the column. At every step it removes the observations of the kind and observes the node of every group that has a member observed, other than as an anchor where any member is, its values in every column reduced over the members that hold a value in it by the aggregate stated, applied to the encoded values, and present where any member holds one. A node of the kind without a value in the column is in no group: it is removed with its edges, as a filter of nodes removes them. Every edge with an end in a group follows it: an edge between two members of one group becomes a loop of its node, and every relation that joins the contracted kind joins the added kind as well. An absent row follows the group its end last belonged to, and stays only where no other edge of the contracted pair is still live. Parallel edges with an end in a group are kept or reduced as `graphspec.relation_forms` reduces them. A reference of a task to a member at the time of the task becomes the node of its group, and one to a node of the kind without a value at that time stops naming the reference, the task and the column. It carries the edges still live under each contracted pair, the last group of every member and the state of removing absent rows left without an earlier present row; it draws nothing; it counts nothing. An entry after it that names the contracted kind, with no merge between them that may bring nodes of it back, would derive nothing for it, so the pipeline stops when it is stated, naming that entry, the kind and the contraction. A contraction of a kind whose nodes a target names, the node a `link` links to or the members of a reference a ranking or a set of nodes lists, stops the pipeline when it is stated, naming the entry, the kind and the kind of task, as does `graphspec.nodes` removing such a kind; a search never offers either.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `contract/grouping` | `<kind>:<column>`, every kind of node with every column the bank states for it; a search offers none the extra `unvalued` of the bank names, and a pipeline stating one is refused naming the kind, the column and the number of nodes without a value | The kind contracted and the column that groups it. |
| `contract/aggregate` | mean, sum, min, max | How the values of the members reduce to the values of their group. |
| `contract/parallel` | keep, first, last, sum, mean, min, max | How parallel edges between groups and other nodes reduce. |

15. Product of relations and its closure, `graphspec.product`

Adds a relation named after its entry holding, at every step, the product of the relations of a stated path: an edge from a node to another wherever a walk leads from the first to the second through the steps of the path, each step a relation of the bank read in a direction, at that step. A step is `<relation>:<direction>`, the direction forward (from source to target), backward (from target to source) or both; a relation without direction is read both ways; `*:both` is every relation of the bank read without direction, alone in its path. A step joins two nodes once, whatever the number of their parallel facts. A path of two steps joins at the nodes where the first ends and the second begins. The product holds no edge from a node to itself; self loops are a form of `graphspec.relation_forms`. Under a closure of length k it also holds the pairs reached by walks of up to k products of the path, each labelled by the shortest number of products that reaches it in a column `<entry>.length`; the closure is offered where the kinds the path ends at are kinds it starts from. A path whose reverse is itself (`visits:forward,visits:backward`, `*:both`, any step read both ways) gives a relation without direction, each pair once, the lower entity first; any other a directed one. Where every step names a column of numbers of its relation, `<relation>:<direction>:<column>`, the relation holds in `<entry>.value` an aggregate of the values along the walks: product, the sum over walks of the product of the values along each (sum over walks of all lengths up to k under a closure); shortest, the least over walks of the sum along each; widest, the greatest over walks of the least along each; parallel facts of a step reduce as the walks do, by sum, least or greatest, and a fact without a value in the column takes no part. A path without columns may count its walks instead, in `<entry>.paths`, parallel facts counted once. Added columns are numbers encoded by the encoder the entry states, fitted as every added column is. A pair is stated at every step it holds and gets an absent row at the step after each run ends, as derived relations are; the pairs live at the end of a segment and, under a cap, the runs of the relations it reads are carried from segment to segment. It names no kind of node: it follows the relations as the registry of the pipeline holds them when it runs, the kinds a contraction joins to them included, and derives nothing where no fact of them is left. It counts paths, and under the forms of storage of set edges counts them as stored. It draws nothing; ties of the cap are broken by tokens. A path holds one or two steps, through the relations of the bank and the relations the entries before it in the pipeline added, never a relation of candidates (`graphspec.negatives`), so that every path offered is valid and their number stays near the square of the number of relations; a longer path is made by chaining products, a later entry naming the relation of an earlier one in its path, as `first:both,first:both` gives the walks of four steps where `first` is `visits:forward,visits:backward`. A stated pipeline is checked against the registry the entries before each entry grew (`pipeline.resolved`, with `pipeline.Growing`). A search offers the paths through the relations of the bank alone: its space is the same for every draw, and the surrogates encode a value only among the values that space holds. `graphspec.derive` projects, closes and diffuses through the same functions of `operations/paths.py`.

| Parameter | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `product/path` | every path of one step, and of two steps whose first ends where the second begins, without columns or with a column on every step, through the relations of the bank and, in a stated pipeline, those the entries before it added | The steps of the product. |
| `product/length` | 1–3 | The length of the closure; 1 is the product alone. Offered where the path's end kinds are kinds it starts from. |
| `product/paths` | off, on | Whether the relation counts its walks. Offered for paths without columns. |
| `product/aggregate` | product, shortest, widest | How values combine along a walk and across walks. Offered for paths with columns. |
| `product/cap` | 0–64 | At most that many edges of each step meet at a node where the path joins, those whose run began latest, ties by the token of their other end, parallel facts counted once; 0 keeps all. Offered for paths of two steps. |
| `product/encoder` | the encoders of numbers, no default | The encoder of every column it adds. Offered where it adds one. |

16. Negative sampling of links and of sets, `graphspec.negatives`

Draws, at every step, what is absent: edges of a relation of the bank that the graph does not hold, or sets of a kind of set edge that it does not hold, for tasks about what is absent. It reads the splits trained on and scored on, and nothing of a split it is not given. What it draws are candidates, never messages: it adds its relations as candidates (`Added.candidates`), which the registry holds apart from its relations, in `Registry.candidates` and `Registry.by_candidate`, with codes of their own from 0, so they take no code, parameter, weight or option of a relation; it leaves the graph as it was given, and hands every drawn edge to `carry.candidate(code, step, source, target)`, so that no operation after it, no representation and no family reads them. A variant whose registry holds candidates keeps them beside its arrays, in `candidate_code`, `candidate_step`, `candidate_source` and `candidate_target`, one row per edge drawn, with the steps and entities of the derivation, ordered by step, code, source and target, as drawn: an entry after it that removes or contracts a node does not change them. `segments.apart` gives the derived graph of one graph with its candidates. Links: it adds as candidates a directed relation named after its entry, from the node kept to the node drawn, joining the kinds the relation joins (both ways for a relation without direction). For every present edge of the relation at the step, read in its direction and both ways for a relation without direction, its source draws the stated number of targets, so that a node kept draws that number times its distinct targets at the step; or, for a task, for every node the stated reference of a labelled task of a split trained on or scored on names at the task's step, the node draws the stated number, tasks that name one node at one step sharing them. The candidates of a node are the nodes observed at the step, other than as anchors, of the kinds the relation joins it to, never the node itself, never a node it holds a present edge of the relation with at the step, and, for a task, never a node a task naming it references or links to, so that a task's positive is never drawn and the task has its count of negatives wherever the rule gives enough nodes; a node with fewer candidates than its number draws them all. Sets: it adds a kind of node named after its entry, numbered before the derivation begins, `count` for every set of the kind that a graph given to the derivation holds, and as candidates a directed relation `<entry>.<role>` for every role of the kind, from the added kind to the kinds of the members. For every set observed at the step, other than as an anchor, or for a task every such set the stated reference names, it draws the stated number of negative sets, each the set with one member replaced by a node of the member's kind observed at the step that is not a member of the set, with the replaced member's role, and never a set of the kind present at the step; a negative set is a node of the added kind, never observed, with a candidate edge to each of its members at the step. The sets must be stored as nodes, and a graph that stores them as pairs stops naming the kind. A rule gives every candidate a weight, and the number wanted of each node or set is drawn among the candidates of positive weight as those with the largest logarithm of their weight plus a Gumbel number, drawn by `hashing.unit(seed, "graphspec.negatives/links", ...)` keyed by the token of the relation's name, the graph and time of the step, the token of the node kept and of the candidate (for sets `graphspec.negatives/sets`, by the graph and time of the step, the tokens of the set, of the role, of the member replaced and of the candidate), ties cut by the candidate's token; the rank of a negative set gives its node. Candidates hold the step they are drawn at, without values and without absent rows. It carries nothing from segment to segment. It names the kind of set edge it draws for, and no kind for links.

Rules are registered in `graphspec.NEGATIVES` as `graphspec.operations.negatives.Rule(parameters, weights, version)`, `version` a whole number from 1 raised whenever its code changes, which the key of every variant drawing with it holds: `parameters(registry)`, the parameters of the rule, each named `negatives/<rule>/<name>`, offered while the rule is chosen; `weights(near, steps, anchors, candidates, settings)`, a weight of at least zero for every candidate, from `near`, the facts of the segment (`near.degree(steps, nodes)`, the facts of the relation drawn for, or of the roles of the kind of set edge, that each node is an end of at its step; `near.within(steps, anchors, candidates, hops)`, whether a candidate lies within a number of hops of its node or set over every present fact of its step, either way; `near.tokens`, the token of every node), the step, the node kept or the set of every candidate, and the settings, through which it reads its parameters. A segment draws all its steps at once, its candidates the nodes observed at each step. A rule registered from outside the library is drawn as the shipped ones are. Shipped: `uniform`, every candidate alike; `degree`, in proportion to its degree in the relation, or to the number of sets of the kind it is a member of, at the step; `hops`, alike among the candidates within `negatives/hops/within` hops.

|---|---|---|
| `negatives/of` | `link:<relation>`, `link:<relation>:<reference>`, `set:<kind>`, `set:<kind>:<reference>` | What it draws: absent edges of a relation stated or a table of relations taken from a column, or absent sets of a kind of set edge, offered where the form of storage of the kind makes its set edges nodes; with a reference, for the node or the set the reference of a labelled task names, the references offered being those of nodes alone whose kinds the relation starts from, or that hold the kind of set edge. |

**Representations.** `groups.base` always produces the base arrays of a segment, and `base.represented` adds the groups a model reads.

Base arrays:

- snapshots: `x_ref` per observation into the feature table `x_table`, the `obs_x` of the views of the variant's graphs in order, widened to the registry of the pipeline, followed by the rows of values its edits and operations added, segment by segment; `entity`, `step`, `obs_period` and `snapshot_offsets` per observation; `observation_ids` and `observation_offsets` per entity; `entity_kind`, all zero when nodes are untyped; relation edges `spatial` as source observation, target observation and code, with `spatial_step`, `spatial_attribute_row` and `attributes_<table>`, one array of values for every table whose relations carry values
- rows, one per task of a kind that states a target: `row_task`, `row_step`, `row_kind`, `row_index`, `key`, `signals`, and `row_left`, true where the target of the task is a `link` and the graph after the pipeline does not hold the node it starts from or the node it links to at its step, other than as an anchor
- references of the rows: `member_row`, `member_name`, `member_entity`, `member_observed`, `member_place`, one entry per node a reference names, an edge giving its two ends, ordered by row, then by the position of the name among the references of the schema, then by place; `member_place` is twice the place of the member plus the end of its edge for a sort that keeps order, and 0 for any other; `whole_row` and `whole_name` for references to the graph itself. Nothing is kept by position: no family reads a reference by its place in the kind.
- tasks: `task`, the features of every task and the attributes of its graph, and for the tasks seen as events `event_first`, `event_task` and `event_step`
- `events`: one row per observation change, relation change or task seen as an event. Observations and facts of step s sit at tick 2s and the tasks of step s at tick 2s+1; the row of a task holds its place in `task`.
- periods: `period`, and the index `period_starts`, `period_ends`, `period_numbers`, `period_membership`, `period_observation_keys`; `affinity_keys` and `affinity_counts`, per task seen as an event ordered by its first node and tick, the counts by kind of the tasks seen as events that node was first of up to and including it; `affinity_steps`, per step, the counts by kind of the tasks seen as events before it
